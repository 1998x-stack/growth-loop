# Evidence Base — professional references and limits

Growth Loop is an **evidence-informed practical synthesis**, not a clinically validated instrument or a universally proven growth algorithm. Cite original scholarly work for the relevant mechanism, rather than claiming a citation validates the entire framework.

## Evidence map

### 1. Goal specificity and challenge

**Locke, E. A., & Latham, G. P. (2002).** Building a practically useful theory of goal setting and task motivation: A 35-year odyssey. *American Psychologist, 57*(9), 705–717. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705

- **Relevant:** clear, challenging goals can influence performance; effects have moderators including ability, commitment, task complexity, feedback.
- **Operational use:** specify an observable delivery standard, rather than "be better."
- **Not established here:** a given 14-day duration or a fixed challenge level maximizes everyone's growth.

### 2. Feedback quality

**Hattie, J., & Timperley, H. (2007).** The power of feedback. *Review of Educational Research, 77*(1), 81–112. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487

- **Relevant:** feedback can be positive or negative; form, level and circumstances matter. A task/process-specific signal is often more useful than vague praise.
- **Operational use:** ask where performance is going, how current performance compares, and what to do next.
- **Boundary:** primarily educational contexts; transfer to every career setting requires judgment.

### 3. Deliberate practice — value and limits

**Ericsson, K. A., Krampe, R. T., & Tesch-Römer, C. (1993).** The role of deliberate practice in the acquisition of expert performance. *Psychological Review, 100*(3), 363–406. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.100.3.363

**Macnamara, B. N., Hambrick, D. Z., & Oswald, F. L. (2014).** Deliberate practice and performance in music, games, sports, education, and professions: A meta-analysis. *Psychological Science, 25*(8), 1608–1618. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614535810

- **Relevant:** practice designed for improvement matters, but it does not explain all performance variance, and associations vary across domains.
- **Operational use:** train an identifiable subskill, obtain feedback, distinguish practice from repetition.
- **Not established:** anyone can become world-class through a fixed number of hours.

### 4. Translating intentions into action

**Gollwitzer, P. M., & Sheeran, P. (2006).** Implementation intentions and goal achievement: A meta-analysis of effects and processes. *Advances in Experimental Social Psychology, 38*, 69–119. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1

- **Relevant:** plans that specify when/where/how ("if situation X, then action Y") can improve follow-through.
- **Operational use:** make the first action observable and tied to a realistic context.
- **Boundary:** planning does not repair a low-value goal or a missing resource.

### 5. Autonomy, competence and social context

**Deci, E. L., & Ryan, R. M. (2000).** The "What" and "Why" of goal pursuits: Human needs and the self-determination of behavior. *Psychological Inquiry, 11*(4), 227–268. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327965PLI1104_01

- **Relevant:** autonomy, competence and relatedness are important in self-determination theory.
- **Operational use:** co-design objectives, include support, avoid coercion or shame.
- **Boundary:** not an endorsement of a specific strengths × market × motivation multiplication formula.

### 6. Habit and context

**Wood, W., & Neal, D. T. (2007).** A new look at habits and the habit–goal interface. *Psychological Review, 114*(4), 843–863. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.114.4.843

- **Relevant:** habits are linked to repeated responses in contexts, not only continuous willpower.
- **Operational use:** preserve environmental cues and review rituals for learning.
- **Boundary:** not all meaningful creative or career decisions can be automated into habits.

### 7. Small experiments and iterative improvement

**Institute for Healthcare Improvement (IHI).** Model for Improvement: Testing Changes — PDSA Cycles. https://www.ihi.org/library/model-for-improvement/testing-changes

- **Relevant:** Plan–Do–Study–Act emphasizes small, local tests and evidence-driven adjustment.
- **Operational use:** make reversible experiments and record predicted versus observed outcomes.
- **Boundary:** a process-improvement method, not direct proof of optimal individual life planning.

## Important distinction: evidence vs inference

| Growth Loop element | Status |
|---|---|
| Clear goals, high-quality feedback, contextual practice, implementation intentions | Informed by the sources above, conditional on context |
| Three horizons (short/mid/long), three outcome types, three questions | **Original organizing synthesis**, not a published validated scale |
| Intersection: advantage × external value × sustained motivation | **Strategic heuristic**; do not claim the Venn overlap is a scientifically measured construct |
| Challenge intensity vs effective learning inverted-U curve | **Illustrative sketch only**; not a universal empirical function or safe stress prescription |
| Stop / adjust / explore / continue gate | **Decision-support heuristic** informed by iterative experimentation |
| Psychological avoidance explanation | **Possible hypothesis** for individual situations, never a diagnosis on user text alone |

## Source hygiene instructions for agents

- Attribute the specific mechanism to its specific reference.
- Do not invent empirical effect sizes, sample sizes or personal outcomes.
- If not actually able to open a paper, cite the DOI and label the interpretation as a high-level summary rather than pretending to have read the full text.
- Do not use the original question-and-answer narratives or historical anecdotes as controlled evidence.
- Separate **science**, **heuristics**, and **individual assumptions** in any detailed explanation.
